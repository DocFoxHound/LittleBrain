# 12: Neuron catalog ⭐ (where the build starts)

The neuron types for the project, each tied to **real human neurons**. We borrow
*lessons* from the fruit fly's mushroom body, but build **human-style** neurons.

## The six types

| Our type | Human neuron it's based on | Plasticity | Memory span | Connections | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Workhorse** (the template) | **Pyramidal neuron** (cortex, hippocampus); about 80% of cortical neurons | medium; cascade synapses | long; old values kept deep | many, on branched dendrites | skills, knowledge, associations |
| **Core / anchor** | pyramidal and PV neurons wrapped in **perineuronal nets** (the coatings that close critical periods) | very low | very long | moderate | temperament, deepest values; hard to break |
| **Branching hub** | **layer 5 thick-tufted pyramidal** and other long-range projection neurons | medium | long | very many, across modules | links distant parts of the brain |
| **Transient** | **dentate gyrus granule cells**, especially newborn ones (highly plastic, very sparse) | very high | seconds to minutes | few; rewired often | scratch work, separating similar experiences; recycled ("fire and forget") |
| **Inhibitory** | **PV** (fast, controls output timing), **SST** (gates individual dendrite branches), **VIP** (inhibits the inhibitors, so context can open a gate) | high | medium | local | suppression: change by holding back, not erasing; choosing which branches are active |
| **Modulatory** | **dopamine** (VTA/SNc), **noradrenaline** (locus coeruleus), **acetylcholine** (basal forebrain), **serotonin** (raphe) | — | — | broadcast | emotion and global state; sets everyone's learning |

Note: "core" is a *condition* a neuron can reach (heavily consolidated, locked by
perineuronal nets) more than a separate cell type. That could let neurons *become* core with age.

## Lessons from the mushroom body
The fly's Kenyon cell is the mushroom body's main neuron. What it teaches:
1. **Sparse coding:** each Kenyon cell listens to a random handful of inputs and has a high threshold. One inhibitory neuron (APL) keeps only about 5–10% active at once. Sparse activity means **each memory touches few synapses**, so memories interfere less.
2. **Coincidence = learning:** an output synapse changes only when the cell was active **and** dopamine arrives. Learning is gated by a third factor.
3. **Compartments with their own rules:** different dopamine compartments learn at different speeds and keep memories for different lengths of time, on the *same* cell's output.
4. **Both directions:** learning can strengthen or weaken connections; "bad" and "good" are written in different places.
5. **Small and complete:** about 2,000 cells handle short- and long-term memory.

## The human equivalents of those lessons
- Sparse coding → **dentate gyrus**, and cortex with PV inhibition.
- Three-factor learning → dopamine, acetylcholine and noradrenaline acting on cortical and hippocampal synapses.
- Compartments → **pyramidal dendrites**: separate branches that each learn and store separately (Cichon & Gan 2015). Different tasks are stored on **different branches**, and SST inhibition decides which branch is listening.
- One-shot learning → **behavioral timescale plasticity (BTSP)** in hippocampal pyramidal cells (Bittner et al. 2017): one strong "plateau" event writes a memory in a single try.
