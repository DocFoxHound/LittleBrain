# 06: Brain regions: what they do and how they learn

A neuroscience reference, kept separate from any AI mapping (that's in note 05).
Organized from oldest and deepest to newest and outermost.

**Confidence:** ● well established · ◐ broadly accepted, details argued · ○ debated or speculative.
Every region does more than one thing, and nothing works alone. "Function" here means
"what it is best known for," not "the only thing it does."

---

## How brains learn: the main mechanisms

| Mechanism | What it is | Where it shows up | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hebbian plasticity** | "Cells that fire together wire together." Connections strengthen when both sides are active together (LTP) and weaken when they're out of step (LTD). Timing matters (STDP). | everywhere, especially cortex and hippocampus | ● |
| **Reward-driven learning** | A dopamine burst means "better than expected" and a dip means "worse than expected." This chemical acts as a third factor that decides which recent connections change. | basal ganglia, prefrontal cortex | ● |
| **Error-correction learning** | A "teacher" signal reports the mistake, and connections adjust to reduce it. | cerebellum (climbing fibers carry the error) | ● |
| **Fast associative / fear conditioning** | One strong experience can link a cue to a feeling ("this sound means danger"). | amygdala | ● |
| **One-shot episodic learning** | A whole event is stored after a single exposure. | hippocampus | ● |
| **Replay and consolidation** | During sleep and rest, the hippocampus replays recent events, and the cortex slowly absorbs them as general knowledge. | hippocampus → cortex | ◐ |
| **Predictive coding** | The brain constantly predicts its inputs and learns mainly from the surprises. | cortex (as a general theory) | ○ |
| **Neuromodulation** | Brain-wide chemicals change *how* every region learns and behaves: how fast, how far ahead, how exploratory. | whole brain | ◐ |
| **Overgrowth then pruning** | Infants grow far more connections than adults keep. Experience decides which survive ("use it or lose it"). | whole brain, peaks in childhood | ● |
| **Critical (sensitive) periods** | Windows when a region learns certain things easily, such as vision in infancy or speech sounds before about 1 year. | sensory cortex, language | ● |
| **Myelination order** | Insulation of nerve fibers matures in sequence: brainstem and sensory/motor areas first, prefrontal cortex last (into the mid-20s). | whole brain | ● |

**Key point for this project:** every part experiences everything at once, but **they
don't all come online at once.** Development is staged. Old survival systems work at
birth, sensory and motor systems mature in the first years, and planning and
self-control mature last. That staging may be part of *why* it works.

## What a newborn arrives with (built in)

- **Reflexes:** rooting (turning toward a touch on the cheek), sucking, grasping, startle (Moro), stepping. ●
- **Crying:** an innate distress signal driven by the brainstem/midbrain (periaqueductal gray). ●
- **Preferences:** face-like patterns; the mother's voice and smell; sweet over bitter. ●
- **Hearing all speech sounds:** babies can tell apart sounds from every language, then narrow to their own language's sounds by about 10–12 months. ●
- **"Core knowledge"** (Spelke): early expectations about objects (they persist and don't pass through each other), about agents (they have goals), about rough quantity, and about space. ◐
- **Drives:** hunger, thirst, temperature, comfort, sleep, the need to be close to a caregiver (attachment). ●

## Hindbrain (oldest)

**Medulla oblongata** ●
- *Does:* breathing, heart rate, blood pressure, swallowing, vomiting, coughing.
- *Learns:* barely; almost entirely built in.

**Pons** ●
- *Does:* relays signals between cortex and cerebellum; involved in sleep, including REM dreaming; contains the **locus coeruleus** (noradrenaline: alertness, vigilance, the "something's up" signal).
- *Learns:* the locus coeruleus adjusts its baseline with stress and experience. ◐

**Cerebellum** ●
- *Does:* coordination, balance, timing, smooth movement, adjusting predictions about movement. Increasingly linked to thinking and language timing too. ◐
- *Learns:* **error correction.** It predicts what a movement will feel like, compares that with what actually happens, and adjusts. This is the clearest example of a "teacher signal" in the brain.

**Reticular formation** ●
- *Does:* a network through the brainstem that controls wakefulness and general arousal.
- *Learns:* adjusts to repeated stimuli (habituation), otherwise largely fixed.

## Midbrain

**Periaqueductal gray (PAG)** ●
- *Does:* survival behaviors (fight, flight, freezing), pain suppression, and **producing calls, including crying**.
- *Learns:* mostly built-in patterns, which higher regions can tune.

**Superior and inferior colliculi** ●
- *Does:* fast reflexive orienting: turning eyes and head toward a sudden sight (superior) or sound (inferior).
- *Learns:* aligns the visual, sound and touch maps during development.

**Ventral tegmental area (VTA) and substantia nigra** ●
- *Does:* the main **dopamine** sources. VTA: motivation, wanting, reward learning. Substantia nigra: starting movements (damaged in Parkinson's).
- *Learns:* their firing *becomes* the learning signal: "better or worse than expected."

**Raphe nuclei** ◐
- *Does:* the main **serotonin** source. Affects mood, patience (waiting for later rewards), sleep, appetite, and how strongly things feel threatening.
- *Learns:* baseline shifts with stress and experience. ○

## Forebrain: deep structures

**Thalamus** ●
- *Does:* the main relay station. Nearly all senses (except smell) pass through it to the cortex. It gates attention and helps set consciousness and sleep states.
- *Learns:* its connections to the cortex are refined by early experience. ◐

**Hypothalamus** ●
- *Does:* keeps the body in balance: hunger, thirst, body temperature, sleep cycles, sex drive, stress response (via the pituitary gland and the HPA axis that produces cortisol). Produces **oxytocin** (bonding, caregiving, trust) and vasopressin.
- *Learns:* drives are built in, but their set points and triggers can be conditioned.

**Basal ganglia** ●
- *Parts:* striatum (caudate, putamen, nucleus accumbens), globus pallidus, subthalamic nucleus, substantia nigra.
- *Does:* **chooses** which competing action, thought or habit goes ahead and holds the others back. Turns repeated behavior into habits. The nucleus accumbens handles motivation and "wanting."
- *Learns:* **from reward**, driven by dopamine.

**Basal forebrain** (e.g. nucleus basalis) ◐
- *Does:* the main **acetylcholine** source. Sharpens attention and marks "pay attention, learn this now."
- *Learns:* sets when other regions learn; less is known about how it learns itself.

## The limbic system (emotion and memory)

**Amygdala** ●
- *Does:* detects threat and importance, especially emotional importance; drives fear, and also reward and social signals. Tags memories as emotionally significant so they're stored more strongly.
- *Learns:* **fast association**. One frightening experience can be enough. Prefrontal cortex can override it later, but that override is new learning and doesn't erase the original.

**Hippocampus** ●
- *Does:* forms new **episodic memories** (what, where, when) and maps space (place cells, plus grid cells next door in the entorhinal cortex). Also imagining future scenes.
- *Learns:* **one-shot**, fast Hebbian learning. Replays memories during sleep. Without it, people can't form new long-term memories of events (patient H.M.).

**Cingulate cortex** ◐
- *Anterior cingulate:* notices conflict and errors, weighs effort against reward, feels the unpleasantness of pain, and of social rejection.
- *Posterior cingulate:* self-reflection and memory retrieval; part of the default mode network.
- *Learns:* from errors and outcomes.

**Septal nuclei** ◐
- *Does:* reward, pleasure, social bonding; helps set the rhythm of the hippocampus.

## Cerebral cortex (newest, outermost)

The cortex is a fairly uniform 6-layer sheet. Different areas specialize mostly because of
**what they're connected to** and what they experience. ◐
This is a strong argument for "same learning rule, different inputs."
Overall it learns **slowly and without labels**, through Hebbian plasticity and possibly prediction.

### Frontal lobe
- **Dorsolateral prefrontal cortex:** working memory, planning, switching rules, abstract reasoning. Matures last. ●
- **Orbitofrontal / ventromedial prefrontal cortex:** how much things are worth to *me* right now; brings emotion into decisions; social judgment (Phineas Gage, Damasio's patients). ●
- **Premotor cortex and supplementary motor area:** planning and sequencing movements. ●
- **Primary motor cortex:** sends movement commands; organized as a map of the body. ●
- **Broca's area** (usually left side): producing speech, grammar, sequencing. ●

### Parietal lobe
- **Primary somatosensory cortex:** touch, pain, temperature, body position; a map of the body. ●
- **Posterior parietal cortex:** where things are relative to me, guiding reaching, attention, number sense. ●
- **Temporoparietal junction:** separating self from other; imagining what others think (theory of mind); redirecting attention. ◐
- **Angular gyrus:** reading, meaning, connecting different senses. ◐

### Temporal lobe
- **Primary auditory cortex:** sound. ●
- **Wernicke's area** (usually left side): understanding speech. ●
- **Inferior temporal cortex:** recognizing objects ("what is it"). ●
- **Fusiform face area:** recognizing faces. ◐
- **Medial temporal lobe / entorhinal cortex:** gateway to the hippocampus; grid cells. ●
- **Temporal pole:** knowledge of people and concepts. ◐

### Occipital lobe
- **Primary visual cortex (V1) and other visual areas:** edges, then shapes, colors and motion, increasing in complexity. Has a critical period: early blindness reshapes it permanently. ●
- Splits into two streams: **"what"** (toward the temporal lobe) and **"where/how"** (toward the parietal lobe). ●

### Insula ◐
- *Does:* senses the body's internal state (heartbeat, gut, breath; "interoception"); disgust; empathy; a sense of "how I feel right now." Links body state to emotion. Many theories place emotional *awareness* here.
- *Learns:* refines its predictions of body signals.

## Connections and large-scale networks

- **Corpus callosum:** about 200 million fibers joining the two hemispheres. Cut it, and each half acts somewhat independently (split brain). ●
- **Lateralization:** language is mostly on the left in most people; prosody (the emotion in a voice), some spatial and face processing lean right. "Left = logical, right = creative" is a myth. ●
- **Default mode network:** active in daydreaming, self-reflection, imagining the future, thinking about others. ●
- **Salience network** (insula + anterior cingulate): decides what matters right now and switches between inward and outward focus. ◐
- **Central executive network:** focused, goal-directed thinking. ◐
- **Mirror neuron system:** cells active both when acting and when watching others act. The broad claims about empathy and language are contested. ○

## The body is part of the system

- **Vagus nerve:** a two-way link to heart, lungs and gut. About 80% of its fibers carry signals *to* the brain. ●
- **Hormones:** cortisol (stress), adrenaline, oxytocin (bonding), sex hormones. They act slowly and broadly, more like long-lasting moods. ●
- **Gut nervous system:** about 500 million neurons; affects mood through the vagus nerve and chemical signals. ◐
- Emotion theories that depend on the body (James–Lange, Damasio, Barrett) all say **emotion is partly the brain sensing what the body is doing.**

## Takeaways for the design
1. Several learning rules, not one: **Hebbian** (cortex), **reward** (basal ganglia), **error correction** (cerebellum), **one-shot** (hippocampus), **fast association** (amygdala), all adjusted by **brain-wide chemicals**.
2. Same experience, different lessons: every region gets the same moments but learns something different from them.
3. **Staged maturation**: survival first, sense and motor next, planning last.
4. Built in at birth: reflexes, a cry, drives, a few preferences, attachment to a caregiver.
5. Emotion needs a body to read: internal states to sense and regulate.
