# 07: When parts turn on, and when they learn

## The words "turn on" mislead
Almost every region is active from birth, many even before it. What changes over time is
how **mature** a region is (wiring, insulation of its fibers, efficiency) and how
**plastic** it is (how easily it changes). A newborn's prefrontal cortex works; it just
isn't good yet, and it's still very changeable.

## Do they all learn at the same time?
Yes. Every region learns all the time, in parallel, from the same experience. But:
- **at different speeds:** the hippocampus is fast, the cortex slow, the amygdala sometimes after a single event;
- **in different windows:** each region has periods when it learns easily (critical periods), then becomes more fixed;
- **with different amounts of learning left:** the medulla barely learns at all; the hippocampus stays changeable for life.

Plasticity declines with age overall, but never reaches zero.

## What decides when a region matures?
1. **Genetic timetable.** When neurons are born, move into place and get insulated follows a built-in order: brainstem → sensory and motor → association areas → prefrontal (into the mid-20s).
2. **Input from earlier stages.** A region can't refine itself until the regions feeding it give it structured input. Primary vision (V1) settles before higher visual areas, which settle before the areas that combine senses.
3. **Critical periods open and close.** A window opens when the region's inhibitory (calming) neurons mature, especially the "PV" interneurons that use GABA (Hensch). It closes when molecular "brakes" form: insulating myelin and mesh-like coatings around neurons called perineuronal nets. Experience during the window decides what gets locked in.
4. **Pruning.** Connections are overproduced, then the unused ones are removed.
5. **Hormones.** Puberty, for example, restructures emotional and social circuits.

## Learning before birth
- **Spontaneous activity:** before the eyes open, the retina generates waves of activity that pre-train the visual system's wiring. The brain trains on data it makes itself.
- **In the womb:** newborns already prefer their mother's voice and the rhythm of their native language (DeCasper & colleagues).

## Moment to moment: a separate question
*Which* regions take part right now is decided by attention, the salience network
(insula + anterior cingulate), the thalamus (which gates what reaches the cortex) and the
brain's chemical messengers (noradrenaline for alertness, acetylcholine for "learn this").

## Design implications
- Give each module its own **plasticity schedule** (learning rate over time), not one global one.
- **Open a module's window when its inputs have settled.** For example, unlock the planner when the perception module's prediction error levels off. Close or slow it afterwards.
- Let all modules learn at once from the same experience, just at different rates.
- **"Retinal waves":** pre-train perception on self-generated patterns before the world is switched on.
- Keep the episodic memory (hippocampus) changeable forever.
- Separate *development* (slow maturation) from *engagement* (the moment-to-moment gate set by attention and global signals).
