# Thalamus: neuron types and proportions

Confidence: ● well established · ◐ supported, numbers vary · ○ uncertain / derived by us.

The dorsal thalamus has only **two neuron classes**: glutamatergic **thalamocortical (TC) relay cells** and GABAergic
**local interneurons**. The **thalamic reticular nucleus (TRN)** is a separate GABAergic shell that wraps it.
Everything else (first/higher-order, core/matrix) is a way of subdividing the relay cells.

Rule of thumb (Arcelli 1997): interneurons are present in the visual thalamus of every species studied; in other
nuclei their number grows with behavioral complexity (bat/rodent → cat → monkey → human). Model: **mouse ~2–6%,
primate ~25–35% of relay-nucleus neurons** (○ for non-visual primate nuclei).

## 2. Relay-cell subdivisions

### 2a. First-order vs higher-order nuclei (Sherman & Guillery)

| | First-order (FO) | Higher-order (HO) |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | LGN (vision), VPM/VPL (touch), MGBv (hearing), VL (cerebellar → motor), AN (mammillary) | pulvinar/LP, POm, MGBd/m, MD, LD, many intralaminar |
| Driver input | subcortical (retina, lemniscus, inferior colliculus, cerebellum) | **cortical layer 5 (ET/PT)** |
| Driver synapses | **~7% of synapses** on relay cells | **~2%** |
| Brainstem ACh/5-HT | depolarizes all relay cells | hyperpolarizes 1/4–1/3 of relay cells |
| Extra GABA inputs | mainly TRN + interneurons | also zona incerta, SNr/GPi, anterior pretectum |
| Fraction of thalamus | minority in primates; most of the primate thalamus by volume is HO (pulvinar + MD) | ◐ |

### 2b. Core vs matrix relay cells (Jones 1998, 2001)

| | Core | Matrix |
|---|---|---|
| Marker (primate) | **parvalbumin (PV)** | **calbindin (CB)** |
| Where | concentrated in principal sensory relays (LGN P/M layers, VPM/VPL, MGBv) | present in **all** nuclei; dominant in intralaminar, posterior, medial nuclei; konio layers of LGN |
| Cortical target | middle layers (L4, deep L3), one area, topographic | **L1**, broad, crosses areal borders |
| Role | specific information relay ("driver" to L4) | diffuse binding/synchrony, tune state |
| Receptive fields | small, sharp | large |
| Proportions | **no reliable %**; varies by nucleus | | ○ |

Mouse equivalent: molecular profiles form a continuum (Phillips 2019: "primary", "secondary", "tertiary" profiles
repeated across modalities) rather than two clean classes (◐).

## 3. Inputs onto relay cells: driver vs modulator

Synapse share onto **LGN relay cells** (cat/primate EM; Sherman's summary figure; Van Horn et al. 2000):

| Input | % of synapses | Driver/modulator | Receptors | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Retina** (driver) | **~5%** (driver synapses 2–7% in thalamus generally; older estimates 10–20%) | driver (class 1) | ionotropic only (AMPA/NMDA) | ◐ |
| **Layer 6 corticothalamic** | **~30–50%** (figure: ~40%) | modulator (class 2) | ionotropic + **mGluR1** | ● |
| **Brainstem** (ACh from PPT/LDT; also NE, 5-HT, histamine) | ~25% | modulator | nicotinic + muscarinic, etc. | ◐ |
| **TRN** | ~15% | inhibitory modulator | GABA-A + GABA-B | ◐ |
| **Local interneurons** | ~15% | inhibitory (incl. dendro-dendritic triads) | GABA-A | ◐ |

The figure's percentages were matched to inputs by us from the layout of Sherman's chapter figure (○ on the exact pairing); the
"drivers 2–5%, layer 6 30–50%" sentence is stated in the text (●).

**Driver (class 1) vs modulator (class 2) properties** (testable):
1. Drivers: ionotropic only; modulators also activate metabotropic glutamate receptors (slow, long EPSPs).
2. Drivers: **high release probability, paired-pulse depression**; modulators: low release, **paired-pulse facilitation**.
3. Drivers: large initial EPSPs, all-or-none; modulators: small, graded.
4. Drivers: little convergence (1–few retinal axons per relay cell); modulators: high convergence.
5. Drivers: large terminals on proximal dendrites; modulators: small terminals on distal dendrites.

## 4. Signature behaviors to model

| Type | Behavior | Test idea |
|---|---|---|
| TC relay | **two modes**: tonic (depolarized, linear relay) vs **burst** (after ≥~100 ms hyperpolarization, T-type Ca²⁺ (Cav3.1) de-inactivates → low-threshold spike + 2–8 spikes); h-current (Ih) gives sag and rebound | already in our tests; add: response linearity in tonic mode, rebound burst after inhibition ends |
| TC relay (FO) | receptive field ≈ that of its 1–few driver inputs | relay output correlates with single driver |
| TC relay (HO) | driven by L5 of cortex; more modulated (lower driver share) | removing L5 input silences it |
| Matrix cell | weak, diffuse output to L1 across areas; large RF | lesion → broad change in cortical gain, not feature loss |
| Interneuron (LGN) | dual output: axonal (F1) + **dendritic GABA release** (F2) in triads with retinal terminal + relay dendrite; locally computed inhibition; receives 8–10 retinal ganglion cells (mouse) | triad: retinal spike excites relay and, via F2, briefly inhibits it (sharpens timing) |
| TRN | burst-prone (strong T-current, Cav3.3), **electrically coupled** (Cx36 gap junctions); generates **sleep spindles (7–15 Hz)** with relay cells; driven by L6 CT and relay collaterals, inhibits relay cells of the same sector (open-loop and closed-loop) | low ACh: TRN–relay loop oscillates at ~10 Hz; high ACh: no spindles |
| TRN PV vs SST | PV cells (throughout, especially core) are **rhythmogenic** and project to FO nuclei; SST cells (mainly outer "shell") project to HO nuclei; only 10–20% co-express both (mouse S1 sector). Core TRN cells burst, shell cells fire tonically (Li 2020; Martinez-Garcia 2020) | PV-TRN drives spindles in FO relay; SST-TRN does not |

## 5. Main connections

- **In:** driver inputs (retina, medial lemniscus, trigeminal, inferior colliculus, cerebellum, mammillary bodies, cortical L5);
  modulators (cortical L6 CT, brainstem ACh/NE/5-HT, TRN, interneurons); extra GABA to HO nuclei from zona incerta,
  basal ganglia output (SNr/GPi), pretectum.
- **Out:** relay cells → cortex (core → L4; matrix → L1), striatum (intralaminar CM/Pf), amygdala (MGBm, PIN),
  hippocampal formation (anterior, reuniens); collaterals → TRN.
- **TRN:** in = L6 CT collaterals + relay collaterals + basal forebrain/brainstem; out = dorsal thalamus only.
- **Transthalamic loop:** cortex L5 → HO thalamus → next cortical area (feedforward driving), parallel to direct cortico-cortical paths.

## 6. Human vs rodent differences

1. **Interneurons**: rare in rodent thalamus (mouse dLGN 5.6%, LP 1.9%, VB ≈ 0) vs common in primates (macaque LGN 25–35%).
   Primate thalamus therefore has far more local inhibition and triadic processing.
2. **Higher-order nuclei expand** in primates (pulvinar, MD); the pulvinar barely exists as such in rodents (LP).
3. Some human thalamic GABA neurons come from the ganglionic eminence (telencephalon), a migration not seen in rodents
   (Letinic & Rakic 2001; ◐).
4. **TRN neuron density decreases** from small mammals to humans (Arcelli 1997).
5. Primate LGN has distinct magno / parvo / konio channels; rodents have a less layered dLGN.
